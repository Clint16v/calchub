Frequently Asked Questions

Compound Interest Calculator

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is calculated on both the initial principal and all accumulated interest from previous periods. Unlike simple interest, it creates exponential growth — the interest itself earns interest. The formula is A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where P is principal, r is annual rate, n is compounding frequency, and t is years.

How often should interest compound for the best returns?

More frequent compounding produces slightly higher returns. Daily compounding beats monthly, which beats annual. However, the difference is smaller than many expect — improving your interest rate has far more impact than optimising compounding frequency. The mathematical limit of infinite compounding uses continuous compounding: A = Pe^(rt).

What is the Rule of 72?

Divide 72 by the annual interest rate to estimate years needed to double your money. At 6% interest, money doubles in roughly 12 years. At 9%, about 8 years. The Rule of 72 works well for rates between 2–20% and is a useful mental shortcut for evaluating long-term growth.

How does compound interest work against you in debt?

Compound interest in debt works exactly like it does in savings — but against you. Credit card companies typically compound daily. A $5,000 balance at 20% APR, making only minimum payments, can take 15+ years to clear and cost over $7,000 in total interest — more than the original debt.

Mortgage Calculator

What is included in a monthly mortgage payment?

A standard mortgage payment covers principal (reducing your loan balance) and interest. Many lenders also escrow property taxes and home insurance, adding these to the monthly payment. The principal and interest portion stays fixed on a fixed-rate mortgage; taxes and insurance can change annually.

How much deposit do I need for a mortgage?

Minimum deposits vary by country and lender — typically 5–20% of the purchase price. Putting down less than 20% usually requires Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) in the US, adding 0.5–1.5% of the loan amount annually to costs. A larger deposit also reduces your monthly payment and total interest paid.

What is the difference between a fixed-rate and adjustable-rate mortgage?

A fixed-rate mortgage maintains the same interest rate for the entire loan term, giving predictable payments. An adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) starts with a lower introductory rate that adjusts periodically based on market indexes. ARMs carry the risk of payment increases but can be cheaper if you plan to sell before the rate adjusts.

How much house can I afford based on my salary?

A commonly used guideline is that your total housing costs (mortgage, taxes, insurance) should not exceed 28% of your gross monthly income, and total debt payments should not exceed 36% (the 28/36 rule). On a $80,000 annual salary, that suggests maximum housing costs of around $1,867/month.

BMI Calculator

What is a normal BMI for adults?

For adults, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered healthy according to WHO guidelines. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25–29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is classified as obese. These are general screening guidelines — BMI does not account for muscle mass, bone density, or body composition.

Is BMI accurate for athletes and muscular people?

BMI is notoriously inaccurate for athletes and muscular individuals. A heavily muscled person can have a high BMI (30+) while having very low body fat percentage. Conversely, someone with a normal BMI may carry unhealthy levels of visceral fat. For athletes, body fat percentage or waist-to-height ratio are better health indicators.

Does BMI differ for men and women?

The BMI formula (weight in kg ÷ height in m²) is identical for both sexes, but healthy body fat percentages differ significantly. Women naturally carry 5–8% more essential fat than men due to reproductive needs. Two people with identical BMIs can have very different metabolic health profiles based on body composition.

How do I lower my BMI?

BMI decreases through fat loss — specifically, creating a caloric deficit while preserving muscle mass. A combination of modest caloric restriction (500 calories/day below maintenance) and resistance training produces the most sustainable results. Crash dieting tends to cause muscle loss, which can worsen metabolic health even as BMI decreases.

Calorie Calculator (TDEE)

What does TDEE stand for?

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure — the total number of calories your body burns in a day, including Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR), physical activity, and the thermic effect of food (the energy used to digest meals, typically 10% of total calories). TDEE is the number to use when setting calorie targets.

How accurate is a TDEE calculator?

TDEE calculators give a useful estimate, typically within 10–15% of your actual expenditure. Individual variation in metabolism, non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT), and body composition mean no formula is perfectly accurate. The best approach: use the calculator as a starting point, track your actual weight change for 2–3 weeks, and adjust calories based on real results.

How many calories should I eat to lose 1kg per week?

One kilogram of body fat contains approximately 7,700 calories. To lose 1kg per week, you need a daily deficit of about 1,100 calories. For most people, 500–750 calories/day deficit is more sustainable, leading to 0.5–0.7kg loss per week while better preserving muscle mass.

Why does my weight loss stall even though I am eating at a deficit?

As you lose weight, your TDEE decreases because smaller body mass requires less energy to maintain. Most people need to recalculate TDEE every 4–6 weeks and reduce intake slightly to maintain the deficit. Water retention from inflammation, hormone fluctuations, and muscle glycogen also cause temporary weight plateaus that can last 1–2 weeks.

Percentage Calculator

What is a percentage?

A percentage is a number expressed as a fraction of 100. For example, 25% means 25 out of every 100, or 0.25 as a decimal. Percentages are used to compare proportions, express discounts, describe growth rates, and measure probability.

How do I find what percentage one number is of another?

Divide the first number by the second, then multiply by 100. For example, to find what percentage 30 is of 150: 30 ÷ 150 = 0.2, then 0.2 × 100 = 20%. So 30 is 20% of 150.

How do I calculate a percentage increase?

Subtract the original value from the new value, divide by the original value, then multiply by 100. For example, if a price rises from $80 to $100: (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = 25% increase.

What is the difference between percentage and percentage points?

Percentage points measure the arithmetic difference between two percentages, while percentage change measures the relative change. If interest rates rise from 2% to 3%, that is 1 percentage point but a 50% percentage increase.

Loan Calculator

How is a monthly loan payment calculated?

Monthly loan payment is calculated using the formula: M = P × [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n − 1], where P is the principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12), and n is the total number of monthly payments. This formula assumes a fixed-rate, fully amortizing loan.

What happens if I pay extra on my loan each month?

Extra payments go directly toward reducing the principal balance, which lowers future interest charges and shortens the loan term. On a 30-year mortgage, adding just $100/month extra from day one can cut years off the loan and save tens of thousands in interest.

What is loan amortization?

Loan amortization is the process of paying off a loan through regular scheduled payments. Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal. An amortization schedule shows exactly how each payment is split between principal and interest for the life of the loan.

What is the difference between APR and interest rate?

The interest rate is the cost of borrowing the principal. APR (Annual Percentage Rate) includes the interest rate plus other fees (origination fees, closing costs), making it a better measure of the true annual cost. APR is always equal to or higher than the interest rate.

Tip Calculator

How much should you tip at a restaurant?

Standard restaurant tipping in the US is 15–20% for good service, with 20% being the most common benchmark for satisfactory service and 25% or more for exceptional service. Tips are calculated on the pre-tax subtotal, though many people tip on the full bill including tax.

How do you calculate a tip quickly in your head?

For a 20% tip: move the decimal one place left (10%) and double it. For a $48 bill: 10% = $4.80, doubled = $9.60. For 15%: calculate 10% and add half. For $48: 10% = $4.80, half = $2.40, total tip = $7.20. Round to a convenient amount.

How do you split a restaurant bill evenly?

Add the total bill, calculate the tip on the subtotal, then add tip to get the grand total. Divide by the number of people. If people ordered very different amounts, some prefer splitting the subtotal proportionally and splitting the tip evenly, or using a bill-splitting app.

What is standard tipping etiquette for other services?

Typical US tipping guidelines: hairdressers 15–20%, food delivery 10–20% (minimum $3–5), hotel housekeeping $2–5/night, taxi/rideshare 15–20%, coffee shop counter service 10–15% optional, bartenders $1–2 per drink or 15–20%. Tipping norms vary significantly by country.

Age Calculator

How do you calculate exact age from a date of birth?

Calculate exact age by subtracting the birth date from today's date, accounting for whether the birthday has occurred this year. Exact age includes years, months, and days. For example, someone born on March 15, 1990 is older on March 15 than on March 14 each year.

How old am I in days, weeks, and months?

To find age in days: count total days from birth date to today (accounting for leap years). To find age in weeks: divide total days by 7. To find age in months: count calendar months, including partial months. A 30-year-old is approximately 10,950 days old (plus or minus a few days for leap years).

What is the average life expectancy globally?

Global average life expectancy is approximately 72–73 years as of 2024 (WHO data). Women live on average 4–5 years longer than men globally. The highest life expectancy countries include Japan, Switzerland, and Australia (85+ years), while some Sub-Saharan African nations have expectancies below 60.

What is the cutoff date for being born in a specific generation?

Widely used generational cutoffs: Silent Generation (1928–1945), Baby Boomers (1946–1964), Generation X (1965–1980), Millennials (1981–1996), Generation Z (1997–2012), Generation Alpha (2013–present). These are sociological approximations, not precise categories — researchers use different boundaries.

Temperature Converter

How do you convert Celsius to Fahrenheit?

Multiply the Celsius temperature by 9/5 (or 1.8) and add 32. Formula: °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32. For example, 20°C: (20 × 1.8) + 32 = 36 + 32 = 68°F. A quick mental trick: double the Celsius, subtract 10%, add 32. So 20°C → 40 − 4 + 32 = 68°F.

How do you convert Fahrenheit to Celsius?

Subtract 32 from the Fahrenheit temperature, then multiply by 5/9 (or divide by 1.8). Formula: °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9. For example, 98.6°F: (98.6 − 32) × 5/9 = 66.6 × 0.556 = 37°C (normal body temperature).

What is absolute zero?

Absolute zero is the coldest theoretically possible temperature: 0 Kelvin = −273.15°C = −459.67°F. At absolute zero, atoms have minimal thermal motion. It is physically impossible to reach absolute zero, though scientists have cooled matter to within billionths of a degree above it.

What temperature does water boil and freeze?

At standard atmospheric pressure (1 atm, 101.325 kPa), water freezes at 0°C (32°F) and boils at 100°C (212°F). These temperatures change with altitude — at 2,000m elevation, water boils at approximately 93°C (199°F), which is why cooking times increase at high altitude.

Sleep Calculator

What is a sleep cycle and how long is it?

A sleep cycle is a sequence of sleep stages (light sleep N1, N2; deep sleep N3; and REM sleep) that repeats throughout the night. One complete cycle lasts approximately 90 minutes on average. A typical night involves 4–6 complete cycles. Deep sleep dominates early cycles; REM sleep increases in later cycles.

How many hours of sleep do adults need?

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine and the Sleep Research Society recommend 7–9 hours of sleep per night for adults aged 18–60. Teenagers need 8–10 hours; school-age children need 9–12 hours; infants need 14–17 hours. Consistently sleeping fewer than 7 hours is associated with increased risk of obesity, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease.

Why do some people feel groggy after 8 hours of sleep?

Feeling groggy after seemingly adequate sleep is often due to waking mid-cycle during deep sleep (sleep inertia). Completing full 90-minute cycles and waking at the end of a cycle produces more refreshed awakening. 7.5 hours (5 cycles) may be more refreshing than 8 hours if the extra 30 minutes disrupts a 6th cycle.

What time should I go to sleep to wake up refreshed?

Count back from your desired wake time in 90-minute increments, then add 15 minutes to fall asleep. To wake at 7:00 AM refreshed: 7:00 AM − 7.5 hours (5 cycles) − 15 min = 11:15 PM bedtime. Or 7:00 AM − 6 hours (4 cycles) − 15 min = 12:45 AM. Consistency in sleep and wake times is as important as duration.

ROI Calculator

What is ROI?

Return on Investment (ROI) is a percentage measure of the gain or loss generated by an investment relative to its cost. Formula: ROI = (Net Profit ÷ Cost of Investment) × 100. An ROI of 20% means you earned $0.20 for every $1 invested. ROI applies to financial investments, business projects, marketing campaigns, and education.

What is a good ROI?

A 'good' ROI depends on the context and time frame. The S&P 500 has returned approximately 10% annually on average over long periods. Real estate typically produces 7–15% annually. For marketing campaigns, an ROI of 500% (5:1 return) is often cited as a strong benchmark. Higher-risk investments should command higher expected ROI.

What is the difference between ROI and annualized ROI?

Basic ROI doesn't account for how long the investment was held. Annualized ROI converts returns to a yearly rate for fair comparison. A 50% total ROI over 5 years is equivalent to roughly 8.5% annualized — very different from the same 50% achieved in 1 year (50% annually).

What are the limitations of ROI as a metric?

ROI doesn't account for time value of money, ignores risk differences between investments, can be manipulated by excluding costs, and doesn't capture qualitative returns (brand value, employee satisfaction). For complex long-term investments, NPV and IRR provide more complete pictures.

VAT Calculator

What is VAT?

VAT (Value Added Tax) is a consumption tax applied at each stage of a product's production and sale, with each business collecting tax on the value they add. The end consumer ultimately bears the full VAT cost. Standard UK VAT is 20%, EU rates vary from 17–27%, and many countries outside Europe use similar systems called GST (Goods and Services Tax).

How do you calculate VAT on a price?

To add VAT to a price: multiply the net (pre-tax) price by (1 + VAT rate). For 20% VAT on £100: £100 × 1.20 = £120. To remove VAT from a gross (VAT-inclusive) price: divide by (1 + VAT rate). For 20% VAT on £120: £120 ÷ 1.20 = £100 net.

Which goods are exempt from VAT in the UK?

In the UK, essential goods are zero-rated or exempt from VAT. Zero-rated items include most food, children's clothing, books, and newspapers. Exempt items include financial services, insurance, and education. Standard 20% VAT applies to most other goods and services.

What is the difference between zero-rated and VAT-exempt?

Zero-rated goods have a 0% VAT rate — businesses can still reclaim VAT on their costs. VAT-exempt goods fall outside the VAT system entirely — businesses selling only exempt goods cannot register for VAT or reclaim input VAT. This distinction matters significantly for business accounting.

Salary to Hourly Calculator

How do you convert an annual salary to an hourly rate?

Divide the annual salary by the number of working hours per year. A full-time worker in the US typically works 2,080 hours/year (52 weeks × 40 hours). A $60,000 annual salary equals approximately $60,000 ÷ 2,080 = $28.85/hour.

What is the difference between gross and net salary?

Gross salary is your total pay before deductions. Net salary (take-home pay) is what you receive after income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and other deductions. In the US, typical combined federal, state, and payroll deductions range from 20–35% depending on income and location.

How do you compare a job offer with a different pay structure?

Convert all offers to the same unit (hourly or annual). Also factor in benefits: health insurance, pension, paid vacation days (each day off has a dollar value = daily rate), bonuses, and remote work savings. A higher salary with poor benefits may be worth less than a lower offer with comprehensive coverage.

What is overtime pay and how is it calculated?

In the US, the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) requires overtime pay of at least 1.5× the regular hourly rate for hours worked above 40 per week by eligible employees. A $20/hour worker earns $30/hour for overtime. Many salaried employees are exempt from overtime requirements.

Calories Burned Calculator

How do you calculate calories burned during exercise?

Calories burned = MET × weight in kg × time in hours. MET (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) is a measure of exercise intensity — walking (3.5 MET), jogging (7 MET), running (10–12 MET), cycling (8 MET). A 70kg person running at 10 MET for 30 minutes burns approximately 70 × 10 × 0.5 = 350 calories.

How many calories does walking burn?

Walking burns approximately 80–100 calories per mile for a 70kg person, or about 250–350 calories per hour depending on pace and terrain. A brisk 30-minute walk at 5 km/h burns roughly 150–200 calories. Incline walking can increase calorie burn by 50% or more.

Does muscle mass affect how many calories you burn?

Yes — muscle tissue burns approximately 6 calories per pound per day at rest, compared to fat's 2 calories per pound. Gaining 10 pounds of muscle increases resting metabolic rate by approximately 60 calories/day. This is why strength training is valuable for long-term weight management.

How accurate are fitness tracker calorie estimates?

Consumer fitness trackers typically overestimate calorie burn by 15–35%. A 2017 Stanford study found errors of up to 93% in energy expenditure estimates from popular devices. Heart rate-based estimates are more accurate than step-count estimates, but individual metabolic variation still causes significant error margins.

Body Fat Calculator

What is a healthy body fat percentage?

Healthy body fat percentages differ by sex and age. For men, 10–20% is generally healthy; above 25% is considered obese. For women, 18–28% is healthy; above 35% is considered obese. Athletes often maintain lower ranges: 6–13% for male athletes, 14–20% for female athletes.

How is body fat percentage measured accurately?

The most accurate methods are DEXA (Dual-energy X-ray Absorptiometry) scan (within 1–2% error), underwater hydrostatic weighing, and Bod Pod air displacement. Skinfold calipers and bioelectrical impedance (home scales) are less accurate (3–8% error) but more accessible for regular tracking.

What is visceral fat vs subcutaneous fat?

Subcutaneous fat is stored just under the skin and is the fat you can pinch. Visceral fat surrounds internal organs in the abdominal cavity and is metabolically active, releasing hormones and inflammatory compounds. High visceral fat is strongly associated with type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and metabolic syndrome, independent of total body fat.

Can you have a normal BMI but high body fat?

Yes — this is called 'normal weight obesity' or 'skinny fat.' People with low muscle mass and high fat mass can have a normal BMI while carrying metabolically harmful levels of body fat, particularly visceral fat. This is why BMI alone is insufficient as a health metric and body composition measurement is more informative.

Debt Payoff Calculator

What is the debt avalanche method?

The debt avalanche method pays off debts in order of highest interest rate first, regardless of balance size, while making minimum payments on all other debts. This is mathematically optimal — it minimises total interest paid and results in the fastest debt-free date.

What is the debt snowball method?

The debt snowball method pays off the smallest balance first regardless of interest rate, using the psychological momentum of quick wins to maintain motivation. After paying off a small debt, that payment amount is added to the next-smallest debt. Research shows it leads to higher completion rates despite higher total interest.

Debt avalanche vs snowball — which is better?

The debt avalanche saves more money mathematically; the debt snowball maintains motivation better. A 2016 study in the Journal of Marketing Research found borrowers who focused on paying off individual accounts rather than reducing total debt were more likely to eliminate all debt. Choose based on your psychological profile.

How long does it take to pay off $20,000 in credit card debt?

At 20% APR paying only the minimum (typically 1–2% of balance), $20,000 in credit card debt could take over 30 years to repay and cost more than $25,000 in interest. Paying $500/month instead eliminates the debt in about 5 years and reduces total interest to around $8,000.

Retirement Calculator

How much do I need to save for retirement?

A common guideline is to have 25× your expected annual retirement expenses saved — based on the 4% withdrawal rule, which suggests you can withdraw 4% of your portfolio annually without depleting it over a 30-year retirement. If you expect to spend $50,000/year in retirement, target $1.25 million.

What is the 4% rule for retirement?

The 4% rule, derived from the Trinity Study, states that retirees can safely withdraw 4% of their investment portfolio in year one, then adjust for inflation annually, with a high probability (around 96%) of not running out of money over a 30-year retirement. It's a guideline, not a guarantee — sequence of returns risk can affect outcomes.

When should I start saving for retirement?

The sooner the better, due to compound interest. $5,000 invested at age 25 at 7% annual return becomes approximately $70,000 by age 65. The same $5,000 invested at age 35 becomes only $35,000. Starting at 25 vs 35 makes the money worth twice as much at retirement.

What is the difference between a 401(k) and an IRA?

A 401(k) is employer-sponsored with higher contribution limits ($23,000 for 2024, $30,500 if 50+) and potential employer matching. An IRA (Individual Retirement Account) is opened independently with lower limits ($7,000, $8,000 if 50+). Both offer traditional (pre-tax) and Roth (post-tax) versions. Max out employer match before other contributions.

Square Footage Calculator

How do you calculate the square footage of a room?

Measure the length and width of the room in feet, then multiply them. For an L-shaped or irregular room, divide it into rectangles, calculate each area separately, and add them together. A room 12 feet × 15 feet has 12 × 15 = 180 square feet.

How many square feet is a typical house?

The average new US single-family home is approximately 2,300 square feet (2023 Census data). Small apartments start at 400–600 sq ft. A typical 3-bedroom home ranges from 1,200–2,000 sq ft. UK homes average about 818 sq ft (76 m²) — significantly smaller than US homes.

How do you convert square feet to square metres?

Divide square feet by 10.764 to get square metres. Alternatively, multiply by 0.0929. For example, a 1,500 sq ft house = 1,500 ÷ 10.764 = 139.4 m². To convert m² to sq ft: multiply by 10.764.

Do you add 10% for waste when calculating flooring?

Yes — for most flooring materials, add 10% to the measured area to account for cuts, waste, and irregular room shapes. For diagonal tile laying or rooms with many angles, add 15%. This extra material also allows for future repairs. Always round up to the nearest package size when purchasing.

Paint Calculator

How do you calculate how much paint you need?

Calculate the total wall area (perimeter × ceiling height), subtract windows and doors (approximately 20 sq ft per door, 15 sq ft per window), then divide by the paint's coverage rate (typically 350–400 sq ft per gallon). Add 10–15% extra. For two coats, double the amount.

How many square feet does a gallon of paint cover?

Most interior paints cover 350–400 square feet per gallon for one coat on smooth surfaces. Porous surfaces (new drywall, bare wood), textured surfaces, and dark-to-light colour changes require more paint. Exterior paints typically cover 250–350 sq ft per gallon due to surface variability.

How many coats of paint are typically needed?

Two coats is standard for most painting jobs. One coat may suffice for touch-ups or when repainting the exact same colour. Three coats are often needed when: painting over a very dark colour with a light colour, priming bare or stained surfaces, or using lower-quality paints.

What is the difference between flat, eggshell, satin, and gloss paint finishes?

Paint sheen affects both appearance and durability: flat/matte (no sheen) — best for hiding imperfections on ceilings and low-traffic walls; eggshell (slight sheen) — good for living rooms and bedrooms; satin (moderate sheen) — ideal for kitchens and bathrooms (wipeable); semi-gloss/gloss — most durable and moisture-resistant, used on trim, doors, and cabinets.

Concrete Calculator

How do you calculate how much concrete you need?

Calculate volume in cubic yards: Volume = Length (ft) × Width (ft) × Depth (ft) ÷ 27. For a 10×10 ft patio 4 inches (0.333 ft) deep: Volume = 10 × 10 × 0.333 ÷ 27 = 1.23 cubic yards. Always add 10% extra for waste. Concrete is typically ordered in cubic yards.

What is the standard concrete mix ratio?

A standard 1:2:3 mix ratio by volume means 1 part cement, 2 parts sand, 3 parts aggregate (gravel). Water is added to achieve workability, typically a water-to-cement ratio of 0.4–0.6. Pre-mixed bags (like 80 lb Quikrete bags) yield approximately 0.60 cubic feet each. 45 bags make one cubic yard.

How long does concrete take to cure?

Concrete reaches approximately 70% of its design strength in 7 days and 99% at 28 days under normal conditions. However, 'curing' (keeping concrete moist) should continue for at least 7 days after pouring to ensure proper hydration. Concrete should not bear significant loads for at least 24–48 hours, and should be protected from freezing for the first week.

What thickness should a concrete slab be?

Standard thicknesses: driveways — 4 inches (residential), 6 inches (heavy vehicles); patios and walkways — 4 inches; garage floors — 4 inches minimum; shed floors — 3.5–4 inches; structural slabs — engineer-specified, often 5–8 inches. Reinforcing steel (rebar or wire mesh) is typically required for driveways and structural applications.
