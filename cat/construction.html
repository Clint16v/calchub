Common Questions — Answered

Quick answers to the most frequently asked calculation questions.

Compound Interest Calculator

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is calculated on both the initial principal and all accumulated interest from previous periods. Unlike simple interest, it creates exponential growth — the interest itself earns interest. The formula is A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where P is principal, r is annual rate, n is compounding frequency, and t is years.

How often should interest compound for the best returns?

More frequent compounding produces slightly higher returns. Daily compounding beats monthly, which beats annual. However, the difference is smaller than many expect — improving your interest rate has far more impact than optimising compounding frequency. The mathematical limit of infinite compounding uses continuous compounding: A = Pe^(rt).

Mortgage Calculator

What is included in a monthly mortgage payment?

A standard mortgage payment covers principal (reducing your loan balance) and interest. Many lenders also escrow property taxes and home insurance, adding these to the monthly payment. The principal and interest portion stays fixed on a fixed-rate mortgage; taxes and insurance can change annually.

How much deposit do I need for a mortgage?

Minimum deposits vary by country and lender — typically 5–20% of the purchase price. Putting down less than 20% usually requires Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) in the US, adding 0.5–1.5% of the loan amount annually to costs. A larger deposit also reduces your monthly payment and total interest paid.

BMI Calculator

What is a normal BMI for adults?

For adults, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered healthy according to WHO guidelines. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25–29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is classified as obese. These are general screening guidelines — BMI does not account for muscle mass, bone density, or body composition.

Is BMI accurate for athletes and muscular people?

BMI is notoriously inaccurate for athletes and muscular individuals. A heavily muscled person can have a high BMI (30+) while having very low body fat percentage. Conversely, someone with a normal BMI may carry unhealthy levels of visceral fat. For athletes, body fat percentage or waist-to-height ratio are better health indicators.

Calorie Calculator (TDEE)

What does TDEE stand for?

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure — the total number of calories your body burns in a day, including Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR), physical activity, and the thermic effect of food (the energy used to digest meals, typically 10% of total calories). TDEE is the number to use when setting calorie targets.

How accurate is a TDEE calculator?

TDEE calculators give a useful estimate, typically within 10–15% of your actual expenditure. Individual variation in metabolism, non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT), and body composition mean no formula is perfectly accurate. The best approach: use the calculator as a starting point, track your actual weight change for 2–3 weeks, and adjust calories based on real results.

Percentage Calculator

What is a percentage?

A percentage is a number expressed as a fraction of 100. For example, 25% means 25 out of every 100, or 0.25 as a decimal. Percentages are used to compare proportions, express discounts, describe growth rates, and measure probability.

How do I find what percentage one number is of another?

Divide the first number by the second, then multiply by 100. For example, to find what percentage 30 is of 150: 30 ÷ 150 = 0.2, then 0.2 × 100 = 20%. So 30 is 20% of 150.

Solar Panel Payback Calculator

How long does it take for solar panels to pay for themselves?

The typical solar panel payback period in the US is 7–12 years, and in the UK 8–14 years, depending on system cost, local electricity rates, and available sunlight. After payback, a solar system typically generates free electricity for a further 10–18 years of its 25-year lifespan, producing $15,000–$40,000 in net savings over that period.

How much do solar panels save per year?

A typical 6 kW residential solar system generates 5,000–8,000 kWh per year depending on location. At an average electricity rate of $0.28/kWh, this represents $1,400–$2,240 in annual savings for households with high self-consumption. The actual saving depends on how much solar power you use directly versus export to the grid, which is paid at a lower rate.
