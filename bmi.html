<h2 style="font-size:1.4rem;font-weight:700;margin:0 0 8px;color:#f0f0ea;">Common Questions — Answered</h2>
<p style="color:#7a7a90;margin:0 0 32px;font-size:0.95rem;">Quick answers to the most frequently asked calculation questions.</p>
<div style="margin-bottom:28px;">
<h3 style="font-size:1rem;font-weight:600;color:#b8ff57;margin:0 0 14px;">Compound Interest Calculator</h3>
<div style="margin-bottom:16px;padding:16px 20px;background:#15151c;border:1px solid #2a2a38;border-radius:10px;">
<p style="font-weight:600;color:#f0f0ea;margin:0 0 8px;font-size:0.95rem;">What is compound interest?</p>
<p style="color:#7a7a90;margin:0;font-size:0.9rem;line-height:1.65;">Compound interest is calculated on both the initial principal and all accumulated interest from previous periods. Unlike simple interest, it creates exponential growth — the interest itself earns interest. The formula is A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where P is principal, r is annual rate, n is compounding frequency, and t is years.</p>
</div>
<div style="margin-bottom:16px;padding:16px 20px;background:#15151c;border:1px solid #2a2a38;border-radius:10px;">
<p style="font-weight:600;color:#f0f0ea;margin:0 0 8px;font-size:0.95rem;">How often should interest compound for the best returns?</p>
<p style="color:#7a7a90;margin:0;font-size:0.9rem;line-height:1.65;">More frequent compounding produces slightly higher returns. Daily compounding beats monthly, which beats annual. However, the difference is smaller than many expect — improving your interest rate has far more impact than optimising compounding frequency. The mathematical limit of infinite compounding uses continuous compounding: A = Pe^(rt).</p>
</div>
</div>
<div style="margin-bottom:28px;">
<h3 style="font-size:1rem;font-weight:600;color:#b8ff57;margin:0 0 14px;">Mortgage Calculator</h3>
<div style="margin-bottom:16px;padding:16px 20px;background:#15151c;border:1px solid #2a2a38;border-radius:10px;">
<p style="font-weight:600;color:#f0f0ea;margin:0 0 8px;font-size:0.95rem;">What is included in a monthly mortgage payment?</p>
<p style="color:#7a7a90;margin:0;font-size:0.9rem;line-height:1.65;">A standard mortgage payment covers principal (reducing your loan balance) and interest. Many lenders also escrow property taxes and home insurance, adding these to the monthly payment. The principal and interest portion stays fixed on a fixed-rate mortgage; taxes and insurance can change annually.</p>
</div>
<div style="margin-bottom:16px;padding:16px 20px;background:#15151c;border:1px solid #2a2a38;border-radius:10px;">
<p style="font-weight:600;color:#f0f0ea;margin:0 0 8px;font-size:0.95rem;">How much deposit do I need for a mortgage?</p>
<p style="color:#7a7a90;margin:0;font-size:0.9rem;line-height:1.65;">Minimum deposits vary by country and lender — typically 5–20% of the purchase price. Putting down less than 20% usually requires Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) in the US, adding 0.5–1.5% of the loan amount annually to costs. A larger deposit also reduces your monthly payment and total interest paid.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div style="margin-bottom:28px;">
<h3 style="font-size:1rem;font-weight:600;color:#b8ff57;margin:0 0 14px;">BMI Calculator</h3>
<div style="margin-bottom:16px;padding:16px 20px;background:#15151c;border:1px solid #2a2a38;border-radius:10px;">
<p style="font-weight:600;color:#f0f0ea;margin:0 0 8px;font-size:0.95rem;">What is a normal BMI for adults?</p>
<p style="color:#7a7a90;margin:0;font-size:0.9rem;line-height:1.65;">For adults, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered healthy according to WHO guidelines. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25–29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is classified as obese. These are general screening guidelines — BMI does not account for muscle mass, bone density, or body composition.</p>
</div>
<div style="margin-bottom:16px;padding:16px 20px;background:#15151c;border:1px solid #2a2a38;border-radius:10px;">
<p style="font-weight:600;color:#f0f0ea;margin:0 0 8px;font-size:0.95rem;">Is BMI accurate for athletes and muscular people?</p>
<p style="color:#7a7a90;margin:0;font-size:0.9rem;line-height:1.65;">BMI is notoriously inaccurate for athletes and muscular individuals. A heavily muscled person can have a high BMI (30+) while having very low body fat percentage. Conversely, someone with a normal BMI may carry unhealthy levels of visceral fat. For athletes, body fat percentage or waist-to-height ratio are better health indicators.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div style="margin-bottom:28px;">
<h3 style="font-size:1rem;font-weight:600;color:#b8ff57;margin:0 0 14px;">Calorie Calculator (TDEE)</h3>
<div style="margin-bottom:16px;padding:16px 20px;background:#15151c;border:1px solid #2a2a38;border-radius:10px;">
<p style="font-weight:600;color:#f0f0ea;margin:0 0 8px;font-size:0.95rem;">What does TDEE stand for?</p>
<p style="color:#7a7a90;margin:0;font-size:0.9rem;line-height:1.65;">TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure — the total number of calories your body burns in a day, including Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR), physical activity, and the thermic effect of food (the energy used to digest meals, typically 10% of total calories). TDEE is the number to use when setting calorie targets.</p>
</div>
<div style="margin-bottom:16px;padding:16px 20px;background:#15151c;border:1px solid #2a2a38;border-radius:10px;">
<p style="font-weight:600;color:#f0f0ea;margin:0 0 8px;font-size:0.95rem;">How accurate is a TDEE calculator?</p>
<p style="color:#7a7a90;margin:0;font-size:0.9rem;line-height:1.65;">TDEE calculators give a useful estimate, typically within 10–15% of your actual expenditure. Individual variation in metabolism, non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT), and body composition mean no formula is perfectly accurate. The best approach: use the calculator as a starting point, track your actual weight change for 2–3 weeks, and adjust calories based on real results.</p>
</div>

<h3 style="font-size:1rem;font-weight:600;color:#b8ff57;margin:0 0 14px;">Percentage Calculator</h3>

<p style="font-weight:600;color:#f0f0ea;margin:0 0 8px;font-size:0.95rem;">What is a percentage?</p>
<p style="color:#7a7a90;margin:0;font-size:0.9rem;line-height:1.65;">A percentage is a number expressed as a fraction of 100. For example, 25% means 25 out of every 100, or 0.25 as a decimal. Percentages are used to compare proportions, express discounts, describe growth rates, and measure probability.</p>
</div>
<div style="margin-bottom:16px;padding:16px 20px;background:#15151c;border:1px solid #2a2a38;border-radius:10px;">
<p style="font-weight:600;color:#f0f0ea;margin:0 0 8px;font-size:0.95rem;">How do I find what percentage one number is of another?</p>
<p style="color:#7a7a90;margin:0;font-size:0.9rem;line-height:1.65;">Divide the first number by the second, then multiply by 100. For example, to find what percentage 30 is of 150: 30 ÷ 150 = 0.2, then 0.2 × 100 = 20%. So 30 is 20% of 150.</p>

<div class="calc-how-it-works">
<div class="calc-how-it-works-title">📐 How It Works</div>
<p>Body Mass Index divides your weight by the square of your height, giving a single number used to screen for weight categories that may carry health risk.</p>
<ol class="calc-how-it-works-steps"><li>Convert height to metres (e.g. 175 cm → 1.75 m).</li><li>Square the height in metres (1.75² = 3.0625).</li><li>Divide weight in kg by that squared height: BMI = weight ÷ height².</li><li>Compare the result to standard bands: under 18.5 is underweight, 18.5–24.9 is normal, 25–29.9 is overweight, 30+ is obese.</li></ol>

<div class="calc-sources"><div class="calc-sources-title">Sources</div><ul><li><a href="https://www.cdc.gov/bmi/adult-calculator/bmi-categories.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CDC – Adult BMI Categories</a></li><li><a href="https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/obesity-and-overweight" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">World Health Organization – Obesity and overweight</a></li></ul></div>
<div class="calc-last-reviewed">Formulas verified against the sources cited above. Last reviewed July 2026.</div>
</div>
<div class="calc-mistakes">
<div class="calc-mistakes-title">⚠️ Common Mistakes</div>
<ul class="calc-mistakes-list"><li>Treating BMI as a diagnosis rather than a screening number. It doesn't measure body fat directly, so muscular athletes routinely show as 'overweight' or 'obese' despite low body fat, and it can understate risk in people with low muscle mass but high fat percentage.</li><li>Using the same thresholds for everyone. The standard 18.5–24.9 'normal' range was developed primarily from European population data — many Asian health bodies use a lower overweight cutoff (23) because health risks appear at lower BMI in those populations.</li><li>Ignoring age. BMI doesn't adjust for the fact that older adults naturally carry more body fat at the same BMI as younger adults, and a slightly higher BMI in older age is sometimes protective rather than harmful.</li><li>Comparing BMI over time without accounting for muscle gain. Someone who starts strength training can see BMI rise even as they get healthier and leaner, since muscle is denser than fat.</li></ul>
</div>
<div class="calc-faq"><div class="calc-faq-title">❓ Frequently Asked Questions</div>
<div class="calc-faq-item">
<div class="calc-faq-q" onclick="toggleFAQ(this)" aria-expanded="false">
What is a normal BMI for adults?<span class="faq-arrow">▼</span>
</div>
<div class="calc-faq-a"><p>For adults, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered healthy according to WHO guidelines. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25–29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is classified as obese. These are general screening guidelines — BMI does not account for muscle mass, bone density, or body composition.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="calc-faq-item">
<div class="calc-faq-q" onclick="toggleFAQ(this)" aria-expanded="false">
Is BMI accurate for athletes and muscular people?<span class="faq-arrow">▼</span>
</div>
<div class="calc-faq-a"><p>BMI is notoriously inaccurate for athletes and muscular individuals. A heavily muscled person can have a high BMI (30+) while having very low body fat percentage. Conversely, someone with a normal BMI may carry unhealthy levels of visceral fat. For athletes, body fat percentage or waist-to-height ratio are better health indicators.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="calc-faq-item">
<div class="calc-faq-q" onclick="toggleFAQ(this)" aria-expanded="false">
Does BMI differ for men and women?<span class="faq-arrow">▼</span>
</div>
<div class="calc-faq-a"><p>The BMI formula (weight in kg ÷ height in m²) is identical for both sexes, but healthy body fat percentages differ significantly. Women naturally carry 5–8% more essential fat than men due to reproductive needs. Two people with identical BMIs can have very different metabolic health profiles based on body composition.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="calc-faq-item">
<div class="calc-faq-q" onclick="toggleFAQ(this)" aria-expanded="false">
How do I lower my BMI?<span class="faq-arrow">▼</span>
</div>
<div class="calc-faq-a"><p>BMI decreases through fat loss — specifically, creating a caloric deficit while preserving muscle mass. A combination of modest caloric restriction (500 calories/day below maintenance) and resistance training produces the most sustainable results. Crash dieting tends to cause muscle loss, which can worsen metabolic health even as BMI decreases.</p></div>
</div></div>
